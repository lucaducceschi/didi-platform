# Researcher Guide

This guide explains how to code articles in DIDI. You don't need any technical knowledge to follow it.

## Key ideas

- **Project:** a study with its own articles, codebook and members. You only see the projects you belong to.
- **Codebook:** the list of codes for a project, which can be nested. Each project has its own.
- **Segment:** a passage of text with a code attached. Only you can change your own segments.
- **Roles:** a **lead** manages a project's articles, members and codes. A **coder** codes articles and can suggest new codes. You can be a lead in one project and a coder in another.

## Log in

Your admin gives you a username and a temporary password. Choose your own under your name at the top of the page, then **Change password**.

## Code an article

1. Open your project. The **Articles** tab lists its articles.
2. Click an article's title to open it.
3. Select a passage with your mouse. A panel opens beside the article.
4. Choose a code from the list, or type a name under **New code** and click **Add**.
5. Add a memo if you want to record a thought.
6. Click **Save segment**. The passage is now highlighted.

The **Corpus search** page at the top searches the whole corpus, and its article pages are read-only. To code an article you find there, click **Open in** your project at the top of the article. If it isn't in your project yet, a lead sees **Add to** the project instead, and a coder can ask their lead to add it.

Spaces at the start or end of your selection are removed automatically. To give a passage two codes, select it again and choose the second code.

When you finish an article, click **Mark done**. You can click **Reopen** to go back to it.

## Read coded passages together

The **Passages** tab lists coded passages from every article in the project.

1. Click a code on the left. Passages under its subcodes are included, unless you untick **Include subcodes**.
2. Narrow them by variety, source, coder or status. You start on your own passages, so choose **Everyone** in **Coder** to see all of them.
3. Click an article's title to see that passage in its article. **Back to passages** returns you to the same list.
4. **Download these passages (CSV)** saves what the filters currently show.

No Ladin speaker has reviewed the Ladin variety labels yet, so check them before comparing varieties.

## Read the highlights

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Colored background | Your segment, in the code's color |
| Dashed underline | Another coder's segment |
| Stronger underline | More than one segment covers this text |

Hover over a highlight to see its code and coder.

**Mine** shows only your segments, and is what you see when you open an article, so you aren't influenced by your colleagues. **All coders** shows everyone's.

## Change or delete a segment

Click your highlight, or the segment in the list beside the article. You can change its code, edit its memo or delete it. You can read other coders' segments, but not change them.

## Working with other people

If someone else has the same article open, a message at the top tells you. You don't need to wait: your segments stay separate, and nothing is locked.

If you and someone else change the same thing, such as a code's definition, the second save is refused with a message, and you see the latest version. Nothing is overwritten without you knowing.

## Messages you may see

- **"…was merged while you were working…"** A lead combined the code you chose with another one. Your segment was saved under the combined code.
- **"You already coded this passage with this code"** That segment already exists, so nothing new was added.
- **"To review"** The article's text changed in a corpus update and your passage can't be placed automatically. It is kept, not deleted. Tell your project lead.

## For project leads

- **Add articles:** on the **Articles** tab, click **Add articles**, search, then add selected results or all new matches. The count appears before anything is added. From the **Corpus search** page, a lead can also click **Add these results to** a project.
- **Remove articles:** click **Remove** beside an article, or **Remove articles nobody has started** to clear mistakes in one go. An article with coded passages can't be removed, so that nobody's work is lost.
- **Add people:** use the **Members** tab. Removing someone keeps their segments.
- **Manage codes:** use the **Codebook** tab to rename, nest, define or color codes. Renaming is always safe.
- **Merge codes:** the confirmation shows how many segments and coders are affected. Merges change other people's work and **can't be undone in the app**, so ask your admin to make a backup before large changes.
- **Export:** **Download segments** and **Download codes** give you CSV files, which open in Excel or R.
